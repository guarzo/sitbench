# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Experienced EVE Online PvE runners reviewing fleet performance on a desktop after a session. They already understand sites, fleet profiles, combat metrics, and EVE terminology. They value fast comparison and information density over introductory guidance.

## Product Purpose

Sitbench turns local EVE gamelogs into confirmed, reproducible site-run records. It helps a runner quickly judge elapsed time, timing deltas, fleet and character contribution, parser coverage, and historical trends while preserving normalized evidence and privacy. Success means the user can understand whether a run improved, why it differed, and how trustworthy the result is without manually reading logs or calculating metrics.

## Brand Personality

Tactical, precise, disciplined. The interface should feel like a serious analytical instrument for an experienced operator: compact, direct, and calm under dense information.

## Anti-references

Do not resemble a generic SaaS dashboard made from identical cards. Avoid oversized decorative metrics, neon gaming aesthetics, glassmorphism, synthetic performance scores, and literal imitation of EVE's interface. Flavor must never obscure data hierarchy or create unfamiliar controls.

## Design Principles

1. Lead with elapsed time and comparison deltas; secondary metrics support that primary judgment.
2. Prefer dense, scannable structures over decorative containers and explanatory filler.
3. Keep provenance, parser coverage, exclusions, and uncertainty visible near the affected result.
4. Make matching-run trends and direct comparisons fast to navigate without losing filter context.
5. Treat participant identity and notes as private capabilities, not assumptions built into every view.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Provide basic accessibility: semantic HTML, readable contrast, visible keyboard focus, labelled controls, keyboard-usable filters and tables, and status cues that do not rely on color alone. Respect reduced-motion preferences. Formal WCAG certification and an extensive assistive-technology audit are outside the initial scope.
